Greetings Friends

We hope you have enjoyed this lovely summer and were able to find some refreshment in whatever ways you enjoy. And, just like that, fall is upon us, with the beautiful sunshine and crisp, clear days! The brilliant fall colours of the trees remind us of God’s many blessings, if we slow down and take the time to consider the splendour He has created for us to enjoy.
As we continue our newsletter series, this fall, we would like to focus on another ministry at St. Paul’s — our Compassionate Care Ministry.

Our Mission: We are a Christ-Centred, worshipping community, serving and leading people to Jesus.

St. Paul’s Ministry Spotlight: Compassionate Care Ministry

Hurting People Find Hope
In our Vision Statements, or the strategies we want to use to fulfill our Mission, #6 says, "Hurting people find hope and healing in the compassion of Jesus". Throughout the Bible, we are called to care for one another. So, the focus of this ministry is to discern how we can care for all ages in our congregation and community, using the spiritual gifts that God has given us.

Diagram: St. Paul’s five ministries surround one mission

Our five ministries surround one mission: Worship/Celebration, Stewardship, Relationships, Equipping, and Compassionate Care, this issue’s focus.

"Therefore, as God’s chosen people, holy and dearly loved, clothe yourselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience." — Colossians 3:12

Jesus Is God’s Compassionate Response
We know so many people who are struggling to find hope today, especially when the challenges come. Jesus is God’s compassionate response to our helpless situation in life. We are invited to follow Jesus and let Jesus display his compassion in us to a hurting world.

Go And Do Likewise
In the Parable of The Good Samaritan, Jesus told the story of the man, beaten by robbers and left on the road, who was ignored and passed by a priest and a Levite. It was only the Samaritan, a bitter rival of the Jews, who not only bandaged his wounds, but paid for his care. When Jesus asked the expert in the law, "Which of these three do you think was a neighbour to the man who fell into the hands of the robbers?" The expert in the law replied, "The one who had mercy on him". Jesus then told him, "Go and do likewise". (Luke 10:25–37)

Go and Do Likewise. Luke 10:25–37.

They Are Not Alone
We are aware that there are hurting people in our church, our neighbourhoods and in our workplaces, and God is calling us to reach out and care for them. This has been at the heart of our Compassionate Response Ministry, where we have offered care and support to those we know who are facing a challenge in their life. We want to remind them they are not alone. As a church, we want to continue to grow this ministry.

"Kindness is the language which the deaf can hear and the blind can see." — Mark Twain

The Gift Of Mercy
Over the past few years, we have learned that God has given some of us the spiritual gift of ‘mercy’. This gift is described as "the divine enablement to cheerfully and practically help those who are suffering or in need, having compassion that is moved to action." Individuals with this gift just seem to know how to reach out to someone, letting them know they care. This God-given gift comes naturally to them.

Your Spiritual Gifts
If you have not completed the spiritual gift assessment, we’d love to take you through that process. It helps us understand the gifts God has given each one of us, to be used and shared to help build His kingdom. We have also learned, when we are using our gifts, we love what we are doing, and don’t get tired. If you don’t yet know your spiritual gifts, but have a heart to care for others, the Compassionate Care Team would love to hear from you, as we seek to grow this team. You can reach out to the current members of the team, which includes Pastor Al, Kim Harrison and Jane MacKintosh.

Mercy: the gift that motivates a believer to feel deeply for those in physical, spiritual, or emotional need and then act to meet that need (Luke 7:12–15, Luke 10:30–37, Matthew 25:34–36).

Did You Know?

A stack of colourful hand-knit and crocheted prayer shawls folded on a wooden chair

Prayer Shawl Ministry — A Handmade Gift Of Comfort, 20 Years And Counting
Did you know that St. Paul’s Prayer Shawl Ministry began in 2005 and celebrated its 20th anniversary in 2025? Through the thoughtful work of those who knit and crochet, prayer shawls have brought warmth, encouragement, and comfort to people experiencing illness, bereavement, and difficult seasons. These gifts have reached people in our community and beyond Ontario. We have heard so many messages of gratitude for these beautiful gifts of care. We want to continue to offer this ministry.

The Power Of Prayer
We have a Prayer Team that prays for those individuals and situations that are raised during our weekly worship service. They also pray, with sensitivity and confidentiality, for those concerns that Pastor Al may become aware of during the week. We believe in the power of prayer and have seen answers to these prayers.

"The power of prayer rests not in the one praying, but in the one hearing." — Marc Brule
