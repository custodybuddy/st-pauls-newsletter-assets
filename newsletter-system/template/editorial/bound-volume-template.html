Greetings Friends

The mornings have turned cold enough that the back door of the church sticks again, and somehow that is how we know the season has changed. The kitchen has been busy since September, the Tuesday coffee hour has grown by a dozen people, and three families who first came to us in the summer are still here.

This edition is about our Compassionate Care Ministry — the quiet work of visits, phone calls, rides to appointments, and prayer shawls knit in living rooms across Ingersoll. It is not work that announces itself. Most of it happens where nobody is watching.

We hope you find something here that makes you want to pick up the phone, or come early on Sunday, or simply pray for someone by name. Thank you for being part of this family.

Compassionate Care

The ministry of showing up

Compassionate Care began with two people and a list of names. It is now a team of fourteen who, between them, made more than four hundred visits and calls last year.

The work is ordinary. Someone drives Margaret to her Thursday appointment. Someone sits with a widower for an hour and talks about hockey. Someone drops off a casserole and does not stay long, because staying long is not always the kindness.

“Which of these three do you think was a neighbour to the man who fell into the hands of robbers? … Go and do likewise.”

Luke 10:36–37

Our Vision Statement #6 calls us to serve, welcome, teach, prepare, and encourage one another. This is where that sentence becomes a Tuesday afternoon.

The team asks for one hour a month. You do not need training, a casserole recipe, or anything to say — only a willingness to knock on a door. To join, or to tell us about someone who could use a visit, speak with Pastor Al after Sunday worship.

Did You Know?

Our prayer shawls travel

Last year the Prayer Shawl group finished sixty-two shawls. Eleven of them left Ingersoll altogether, mailed to family members in four provinces and two countries. Each one takes roughly twenty hours to knit, and each one is prayed over before it is given away.

 

One in four live alone

In Oxford County, close to one in four households is a single person living on their own, and that share is highest among people over seventy-five. Measured against that, a phone call is not a small thing.

A congregation that notices who is missing is doing something no program can replace.
