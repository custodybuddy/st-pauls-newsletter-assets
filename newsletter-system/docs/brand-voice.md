# St. Paul's Brand Voice Guide

**Status:** Draft v1, for Kathy to review. Nothing here is final until she marks it approved.
**Built from:** the three archived newsletters (September 2025, November 2025, Spring 2026) in `newsletters/archive/`, plus the church's own approved phrases. Every rule below cites the words it came from.
**Used for:** social posts (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn), short-video captions, "We Are So Thankful For" write-ups, and any automated draft. Kathy remains the source of truth: when this guide and her copy disagree, her copy wins and this guide gets updated.

How to read the evidence labels:

- **FACT** is quoted straight from a newsletter.
- **PATTERN** appears in two or more issues.
- **HYPOTHESIS** is a reasonable guess that needs Kathy's yes or no.

---

## 1. Who is speaking

St. Paul's speaks as a **church family writing to its own family**, in the first-person plural.

- PATTERN: every issue opens "Greetings Friends".
- FACT: "At St. Paul's, like any family, we realize how important it is to keep in touch."
- PATTERN: "church family" and "faith family" are used instead of "members" or "congregation".

**Rule:** write as "we", speak to "you", and talk about "our church family". Never write as a brand or an organization.

## 2. The five traits

### 2.1 Warm and personal

- FACT: "It has been such a wonderful fall!"
- FACT: "Welcome to spring! It's been a long winter, but we made it through."

Seasonal, everyday openings come before anything serious.

### 2.2 Honest and humble

The church says plainly where it is, without spin or alarm.

- FACT: "It may not be where we need to be in the future, but it's where we are today."
- FACT: "People aren't just walking through our doors."
- FACT: "While we did see increases in our givings in February, we did fall behind in March."

**Rule:** name the hard thing in one plain sentence, then turn to hope. Never hide a problem, and never dramatize one.

### 2.3 Gently inviting, never pushing

- PATTERN: "Perhaps" is the signature move for an invitation.
  - FACT: "Perhaps we could strengthen our support of this very important place in our community?"
  - FACT: "Perhaps God wants St. Paul's to be an answer to prayers in our community..."
- PATTERN: "We'd love to hear from you" (and variants) closes most calls to action.
- FACT: "We prayerfully encourage everyone to do what you can to support our budget."

**Rule:** ask, don't tell. Make one clear, small next step easy to take. No guilt and no urgency language ("act now", "don't miss out", "last chance").

### 2.4 Rooted in scripture and mission

- FACT: scripture is cited with its reference: "The Widow's Olive Oil (2 Kings 4:1-7)", "Feeding the 5,000 (John 6:1-14)".
- PATTERN: every issue repeats the mission statement word for word: "We are a Christ-centered, worshipping community, serving and leading people to Jesus."
- PATTERN: "the hands and feet of Christ".
- PATTERN: every issue closes with "Let everything that has breath praise the Lord." (Psalm 150:6).

**Rule:** connect the news to faith in one line, and always give the scripture reference. Explain; don't preach.

### 2.5 Hopeful and grateful

- FACT: "May we pray that St. Paul's has not yet seen its best days in doing the work of our Lord."
- FACT: "Thank you for serving faithfully, often quietly, and always meaningfully."
- FACT: "...with God's help, we can do amazing things!"

**Rule:** end on hope or thanks, never on a problem.

## 3. Words St. Paul's uses

These come from the archive. Use them naturally, not as decoration.

| Prefer | Instead of |
|---|---|
| church family, faith family | members, audience, congregation (in casual copy) |
| ministry, team | program, department |
| gifts, talents, passions | skills, resources |
| discern, discernment | decide, strategize |
| serve, share your gifts | volunteer for, sign up |
| bless, blessing | help out (when it is about outreach) |
| generosity, stewardship | fundraising, donations drive |
| hope and healing, a hurting world | (no stand-in; these are core phrases) |
| "We'd love to hear from you" | "Contact us", "Submit feedback" |

**Approved key phrases** (repeat exactly, never paraphrase):

- "We are a Christ-centered, worshipping community, serving and leading people to Jesus."
- "When we place our gifts in God's hands, God does remarkable things." (Stewardship theme)
- "People joyfully and generously serve according to the gifts God has given them." (Vision Statement #4)
- "All are welcome!"

## 4. Writing mechanics

- **Canadian spelling:** neighbourhood, travelling, honour, colour. (FACT: "neighbourhoods", "travelling" appear in the archive.)
- **Short paragraphs:** two to four sentences.
- **Exclamation marks:** at most one per post, for real joy ("Welcome to spring!"), never for urgency.
- **Emoji:** the newsletter uses one emoji as a section icon, never inside sentences. For social, use at most one or two, at the start of a line. (HYPOTHESIS: Kathy to confirm for social.)
- **Names:** "Pastor Al" (not "the pastor", not "Rev. Brouwer" in casual copy). Staff and volunteers by full first and last name in thanks.
- **Times and dates:** "10:30 am", "Sunday, May 10".
- **Money:** exact figures from the source, with cents where the source has them. Never round or recalculate a published figure without saying so.

## 5. "We Are So Thankful For"

The archive pattern, which every thanks should follow:

1. **Who:** full names.
2. **What they did:** the specific act.
3. **Why it mattered:** one line on the impact.

- FACT: "Shannon Carlyle, who has stepped away from leadership in the Sunday School after 12 years, including through the pandemic. Thank you, Shannon, for sharing your gifts with our children. You may never know the positive impact you have made in their lives."
- FACT: "Harold and Nancy Matthews, who demonstrated gracious hospitality by hosting our 'Welcome Back Picnic' again this fall."

**Suggesting who to thank** (for the automated extraction): look in bulletins, emails and sermons for people who led, hosted, cooked, taught, drove, stepped down after long service, or reached a milestone. AI only **suggests**; Kathy chooses.

**Privacy rules for thanks on social media:**

- Never name children or teens on social media. Thank the group ("our Sunday School helpers").
- Never mention anyone's health, grief, finances or prayer needs.
- Adults are named on social only when Kathy confirms they are comfortable with it.

## 6. Pastor Al's words

- Quote **word for word** from the recording, checked against the video, with the date of the sermon.
- Attribute as: — Pastor Al, [Sunday date].
- Never edit a quote to make it punchier. If a phrase needs context, add a line before it in our own voice.
- Pastor Al agrees, once and up front, to his sermons being clipped and quoted.

## 7. Platform guide (HYPOTHESIS: to test in the pilot week)

| Platform | Who it reaches | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook | Members, families, people in Ingersoll | 60–120 words | Warm opener, the news, one invitation, the details (day, time, place) |
| Instagram | Younger families, neighbours | 30–60 words plus one image | One strong line first, then the details, then "All are welcome!" |
| LinkedIn | Community partners, local leaders | 80–150 words | The community impact: partnerships, outreach, numbers served |

## 8. Voice test: sample posts for Kathy to grade

These are written from the Spring 2026 newsletter's "St. Paul's Strawberry Supper" event. Kathy marks what she would change, and each change becomes a rule above.

**Instagram (draft)**

> Fresh local strawberries, a delicious supper, and friends old and new. 🍓
>
> St. Paul's Strawberry Supper · Friday, June 19 · 56 Thames St. S, Ingersoll
>
> All are welcome!

**LinkedIn (draft)**

> For generations, St. Paul's Presbyterian Church has gathered Ingersoll around the table. On Friday, June 19, our Strawberry Supper returns, featuring fresh local strawberries and an evening of fellowship open to the whole community.
>
> Events like this are part of how we hope to be the "hands and feet of Christ" in Ingersoll: noticing our neighbours and making room for them. All are welcome.

Notes on the drafts: the time and price of the supper are not in the newsletter ("TBD"), so the drafts leave them out. Nothing is invented.

## 9. How this guide gets better

1. Every automated draft is graded by Kathy: approved as is, or approved with edits.
2. Each edit she makes is turned into a rule or an example here, with the date.
3. The goal is posts that need no edits. The approval step stays permanent anyway: church content touches names, grief and prayer, and a quick "yes" costs less than one wrong post.
