St. Paul’s Ministry Spotlight

Compassionate Care Ministry

Sharing Compassion
 

In our Vision Statements, or the strategies we want to use to fulfill our Mission, #6 says, “Hurting people find hope and healing in the compassion of Jesus”. Throughout the Bible, we are called to care for one another. So, the focus of this ministry is to discern how we can care for all ages in our congregation and community, using the spiritual gifts that God has given us.

We know so many people who are struggling to find hope today, especially when the challenges come. Jesus is God’s compassionate response to our helpless situation in life. We are invited to follow Jesus and let Jesus display his compassion in us to a hurting world.

One Christ-centred community
 

In the Parable of The Good Samaritan,

Jesus told the story of the man, beaten by robbers and left on the road, who was ignored and passed by a priest and a Levite. It was only the Samaritan, a bitter rival of the Jews, who not only bandaged his wounds, but paid for his care. When Jesus asked the expert in the law, “Which of these three do you think was a neighbour to the man who fell into the hands of the robbers?” The expert in the law replied, “The one who had mercy on him”. Jesus then told him,

“Go and do likewise”.

We are aware that there are hurting people in our church, our neighbourhoods and in our workplaces, and God is calling us to reach out and care for them. This has been at the heart of our Compassionate Response Ministry, where we have offered care and support to those we know who are facing a challenge in their life. We want to remind them they are not alone. As a church, we want to continue to grow this ministry.

Over the past few years, we have learned that God has given some of us the spiritual gift of ‘mercy’.

This gift is described as

“the divine enablement to cheerfully and practically help those who are suffering or in need, having compassion that is moved to action”.

Individuals with this gift just seem to know how to reach out to someone, letting them know they care. This God-given gift comes naturally to them.

If you have not completed the spiritual gift assessment, we’d love to take you through that process.

It helps us understand the gifts God has given each one of us, to be used and shared to help build His kingdom. We have also learned, when we are using our gifts, we love what we are doing, and don’t get tired. If you don’t yet know your spiritual gifts, but have a heart to care for others, the Compassionate Care Team would love to hear from you, as we seek to grow this team.

You can reach out to Pastor Al or Kim Harrison, who leads this team.
