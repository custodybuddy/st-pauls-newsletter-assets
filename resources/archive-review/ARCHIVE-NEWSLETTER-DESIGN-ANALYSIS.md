## Shared Newsletter System

- All three issues use production-email patterns: nested layout tables, inline styling, absolute image URLs, and card-like content regions.
- Each issue follows a familiar story arc: opening pastoral message, mission or vision framing, ministry/community content, engagement or events, gratitude, and a church footer.
- Georgia-style serif headings and Arial-style body text create a reliable editorial hierarchy.
- Repeated modules include a dark feature panel, cream or white editorial cards, gold accents, section labels, calls to action, and a dark navy footer.

## Issue-by-Issue Pattern Review

### September 2025

- Most compact, modular foundation: 70 tables, 6 images, and 2 media queries.
- Uses a cream page background, white cards, navy feature panels, teal information accents, and gold highlights.
- Prioritizes vision statements, ministry orientation, and action prompts; it relies more on text and structured cards than photography.
- Ends with compact two-column engagement and event cards, then a high-contrast navy footer.

### November 2025

- Most story- and photography-led issue: 38 tables, 19 images, and no media query.
- Uses a warm seasonal hero, pale mint content cards, slate text, and gold accents. This creates a softer autumn variation of the church visual language.
- Repeats a long-form stewardship narrative using photo cards, pull quotes, verse cards, and a compact events/gratitude ending.
- The visual hierarchy is calm and readable, though the repeated light cards make the issue feel less structurally varied than April 2026.

### April 2026

- Most complete current design system: 70 tables, 12 images, and 3 media queries.
- Applies the strongest navy, gold, cream, and soft-blue system, closely matching the documented St. Paul’s palette.
- Expands the content pattern into more purposeful modules: community-need statistics, ministry highlights, learning/reflection cards, financial snapshots, CTA blocks, events, prayer, gratitude, and a branded close.
- Uses dark panels as deliberate visual pauses and applies gold labels/dividers consistently, giving the long issue a clear scan path.

## Design-System Conclusion

April 2026 supplied useful visual evidence for the current system: it has clear colour discipline, consistent spacing and editorial hierarchy, and a developed set of reusable newsletter modules.

September 2025 contributes a useful compact-card and engagement pattern. November 2025 contributes warm, image-led storytelling for seasonal or feature-led issues. Apply those lessons only through the comprehensive template’s edition types, layout rules, accessibility requirements, and current v3 icon system.
